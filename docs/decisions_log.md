# Decisions log

Every assumption or cleaning decision is appended here (newest at the bottom).

| Date | Phase | Decision | Reason | Impact |
|------|-------|----------|--------|--------|
| 2026-11-20 | 1 | Use `customer_unique_id` as the customer key. | `customer_id` is generated per order, so the same person appears under several `customer_id`s. | All customer-level metrics and joins go through the `customers` table to map `customer_id` → `customer_unique_id`; counting `customer_id` would overstate customers and understate repeat buying. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Analysis window: purchases from 2017-01-01 to 2018-08-31 inclusive (`orders.in_analysis_window`). | 2016 is sparse (4 orders in 2016-09, 324 in 2016-10 on 10 days, none in 2016-11, 1 in 2016-12). 2018-09 and 2018-10 hold 20 orders, none delivered (end of extract). | 99,092 of 99,441 orders in window (329 before, 20 after). 96,211 of 96,478 delivered orders in window. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | 180-day follow-up eligibility: first delivered order on or before 2018-03-04, compared by calendar date. | 2018-03-04 + 180 days = 2018-08-31, the last day of the window, so every eligible customer has a full 180 days of follow-up (right-censoring). | 56,188 eligible customers. Profiling showed 55,973, because it compared the timestamp with 2018-03-04 00:00 (missing 221 customers who first bought later that day) and did not yet apply the first-order rule. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Repeat purchase = a delivered order by the same `customer_unique_id` on a later calendar date than their first delivered order. Same-day additional orders are not repeats. | A second order on the same day is the same shopping trip (e.g. a split basket), not the customer coming back. | Lifetime: 2,788 → 2,004 repeaters (2.99% → 2.15% of 93,094); 784 were same-day only. Within 180 days: 1,756 → 1,112 (3.13% → 1.98% of 56,188); 644 were same-day only. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | First delivered order per customer is taken from the full history; the window is applied to cohorts afterwards. | Otherwise a customer who first bought before 2017 would be counted as new inside the window. | 10 customers with a delivered order in the window first bought before 2017-01-01. They are excluded from window cohorts (93,104 naive vs 93,094 cohort customers). |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Non-delivered orders stay in `orders` with `is_delivered = False`. They are excluded from delivery, review and retention metrics and analysed separately. | Keeps totals honest and supports the cancellation analysis. | 2,963 non-delivered orders (99,441 − 96,478). |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Missing dates are left empty, not imputed. Delivered orders missing a customer delivery date stay delivered for retention but are excluded from delivery-time metrics. | Guessing dates would invent data. | `flag_delivered_missing_customer_date`: 8 orders. Delivered orders missing approval date: 14; missing carrier date: 2 (left empty, no flag column). |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Out-of-order dates are kept and flagged; flagged orders are excluded only from step-timing metrics (approval → carrier → customer). | Purchase → delivery is never negative, so the main delivery metrics are unaffected; the gaps look like recording noise. | `flag_carrier_before_approval` 1,359; `flag_carrier_before_purchase` 166; `flag_delivery_before_carrier` 23. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Cancelled orders with a customer delivery date stay cancelled and are flagged. | Status is the authoritative outcome; the date is contradictory. | `flag_cancelled_with_delivery_date`: 6 orders. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | One review per order: latest `review_answer_timestamp`, ties → latest `review_creation_date` → highest `review_id`. | `review_answer_timestamp` is never empty, has a time of day, and had no ties within multi-review orders; `review_creation_date` tied in 157. | `order_reviews` 99,224 → 98,673 rows (551 removed). 547 orders flagged `flag_multiple_reviews`. 768 orders have no review (no row, score empty). |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Repeated `review_id`s on different orders are kept. | Each copy has identical content but links to a different order, so each (review, order) pair is valid. | 789 repeated review_ids in raw (814 extra rows); after one-per-order, 578 extra rows still share a review_id (kept on purpose). |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Payments and items are each summed to order level before comparing or joining. Revenue comes from item price + freight, not `payment_value`. Orders where they differ by more than R$0.01 are flagged, not changed. | Both tables have several rows per order; joining row to row multiplies money. The cause of the gaps is not proven. | `flag_payment_mismatch` 303 orders; `flag_no_payment` 1; `flag_no_items` 775. Payment rows: `flag_zero_value` 9, `flag_payment_type_not_defined` 3 (excluded from payment-type analysis). |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Products: English category only (`category_name_english`), `'unknown'` for a missing category. Added translations `pc_gamer → pc_gamer` and `portateis_cozinha_e_preparadores_de_alimentos → portable_kitchen_and_food_preparers`. Renamed `product_name_lenght` / `product_description_lenght` to `..._length`. Missing sizes left empty. | Consistent English labels for reporting; source column names are misspelled. | 610 products `'unknown'` (`flag_missing_category`); 13 products in the two newly translated categories; `flag_zero_weight` 4; `flag_missing_dimensions` 2. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Price and freight outliers are kept and flagged; analyses prefer medians and percentiles. | Top items look genuine (high price with matching freight); removing them would drop real sales. | `flag_price_above_p99` 1,117 items (p99 R$890.00); `flag_freight_above_p99` 1,124 (p99 R$84.52); `flag_zero_freight` 383. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Geolocation: drop exact duplicates, drop points outside Brazil's bounding box (lat −34 to 5.5, lng −74 to −34), then one row per zip prefix with median lat/lng, the most common city/state and `n_points`. | Raw has many points per prefix plus duplicates and foreign coordinates; joins need one row per prefix. Low priority, so kept simple. | 1,000,163 → 738,332 (−261,831 duplicates) → 738,299 (−33 outside Brazil) → 19,010 prefixes (5 prefixes lost). `flag_zip_not_in_geolocation`: customers 279 (278 vs raw + 1 from lost prefixes), sellers 7. |
| 2026-11-20 | 2 | Notebooks run with `.venv\Scripts\python -m nbconvert` on the 'Python 3.11 (olist .venv)' kernel. | `python -m jupyter nbconvert` started the system `jupyter-nbconvert.exe` from PATH, which ran on system Python with pandas 2.3.3. | Both notebooks verified on `.venv`, pandas 3.1.1. Convention added to CLAUDE.md. |
| 2026-11-21 | 3 | Cleaned tables live in a dedicated schema `clean` in the `olist` database. `sql/01_schema.sql` drops and recreates only `clean.*` tables (children first, no `CASCADE`). | Keeps cleaned data apart from `public` and later reporting objects; re-runnable without touching anything else. No `CASCADE`, so an unexpected dependent object makes the drop fail loudly instead of being deleted. | 8 tables recreated on every load run; nothing outside `clean` is changed. |
| 2026-11-21 | 3 | The 7 product whole-number columns that pandas wrote as `N.0` (`product_name_length`, `product_description_length`, `product_photos_qty`, `product_weight_g`, `product_length_cm`, `product_height_cm`, `product_width_cm`) are typed `integer`. The loader converts `N.0` → `N` in memory and stops on any other non-blank value. | They are counts, grams and cm. pandas made them floats only because of blanks. `numeric` would hide that they are whole numbers; rounding could silently change data. | All non-blank values were `N.0`, so no value changed. NULLs 610 (name length) and 2 (weight) match the CSV blanks; total weight 75,007,492 g in both. CSV on disk unchanged. |
| 2026-11-21 | 3 | Types: `numeric(10,2)` for money, `double precision` for latitude/longitude, `timestamp` for all dates (including the date-only `order_estimated_delivery_date`, stored as midnight), `text` for IDs and zip prefixes, `boolean` for flags. | Exact money sums; coordinates are measurements, not money; one consistent date type; zip prefixes keep leading zeros. | Database sums of price (13,591,643.70) and payment_value (16,008,872.12) equal the CSV sums; 23,995 customer zip prefixes with a leading zero preserved. |
| 2026-11-21 | 3 | Primary keys as designed (incl. `order_reviews(order_id)`); foreign keys orders → customers, order_items → orders/products/sellers, order_payments → orders, order_reviews → orders. **No** foreign key from customers or sellers to geolocation. | `review_id` is not unique after Phase 2. 279 customers and 7 sellers have no geolocation prefix; an FK would reject real rows. | Load succeeded with all keys enforced; geolocation joined with `LEFT JOIN` in analyses. |
| 2026-11-21 | 3 | CHECK constraints: zip prefixes exactly 5 digits; `review_score` 1–5; price, freight, payment value and installments `>= 0`. | Encode facts found in Phase 2 profiling so future loads cannot break them silently. | No row rejected. |
| 2026-11-21 | 3 | Schema, indexes and all 8 COPYs run in one transaction; indexes are created before loading. | All-or-nothing load. At this size (569,147 rows in total), building indexes before COPY is fast enough and keeps the script simple; for much larger loads, indexing after COPY would be faster. | Loader run twice; both runs committed identical row counts. |
